AM: amplitude modulation

What am I looking at?

Amplitude modulation puts a message m(t) onto the amplitude of a carrier. Everything here is simulated at complex baseband: the carrier sits at 0 Hz, so a transmitted DSB-LC signal is simply 1 + μ·m(t), and the channel's carrier phase offset shows up as a rotation of the I/Q scatter. The spectrum shows the carrier at 0 Hz with the lower and upper sidebands (LSB/USB) mirrored around it, occupying a total bandwidth of 2W.

DSB-LC vs DSB-SC. DSB-LC ("large carrier", ordinary broadcast AM) transmits the carrier as well. That wastes power (the modulation efficiency η is at most 33 % for a tone at μ = 1) but lets a very simple receiver work. DSB-SC suppresses the carrier, so every watt carries the message, but the receiver must regenerate the carrier phase.

Envelope vs coherent detection. An envelope detector just takes |r(t)|. It doesn't care about phase, which is why cheap AM radios use it, but it only works when 1 + μ·m(t) stays positive (μ ≤ 1) and the carrier is present. A coherent detector multiplies by a local carrier with phase estimate φ̂ and keeps the real part. It works for both DSB-LC and DSB-SC, but a phase error Δφ scales the output by cos Δφ, and at 90° the message vanishes.

The threshold effect. With a strong carrier, noise mostly perturbs the envelope's amplitude and the envelope detector matches coherent detection. Once the noise becomes comparable to the carrier (CNR around 10 dB and below), the envelope is dominated by noise, the message is no longer present as an additive term, and the output SNR falls much faster than the CNR. Use "Compute SNR curve" to see the envelope curve peel away from the coherent one.
