BER: Monte Carlo sweeps

What am I looking at?

BER and Eb/N0. The bit error rate is the fraction of payload bits decoded wrong. Eb/N0 is the energy per bit over the noise density: the fair way to compare schemes with different bits per symbol or bandwidth. Theory curves are drawn as lines; each simulated point is a marker with its 95% confidence interval.

Why ≥ 100 errors. A BER estimate from k errors has a relative uncertainty of about 1/√k. With 10 errors the true BER could easily be half or double, but with 100 the interval is about ±20%. Points stop at 100 errors (or a bit / time limit), so low-BER points need many more bits, and the sweep stops early once the BER drops below the floor you set.

Confidence intervals. The error bars are Wilson 95% intervals: if the simulation is right, the theory line should pass through about 19 of every 20 bars. Each point has its own seed derived from the sweep seed, so a rerun gives exactly the same numbers.

Implementation loss is how much more Eb/N0 the real receiver needs than theory to reach the same BER: the horizontal gap at 1e-3 or 1e-4, read from the summary table. Real synchronization, finite filters and estimation noise all cost a fraction of a dB each.

Rayleigh's slope. In a Rayleigh fade, the probability that the channel gain is small enough to cause an error falls only as 1/SNR. So the BER falls one decade per decade of SNR (10 dB): the "diversity slope" of 1 in the summary, while AWGN curves dive ever more steeply. Diversity (more antennas, coding) increases that slope.

Why loop bandwidth has an optimum. A carrier PLL facing phase noise has two error sources. A narrow loop can't follow the oscillator's random walk and cycle-slips, while a wide loop passes more thermal noise into its phase estimate. The BER vs BnT curve is therefore U-shaped, and its minimum moves to wider loops as the phase noise grows.
